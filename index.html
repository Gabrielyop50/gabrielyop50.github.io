📡 aqara-fp400-zone-card - Map Presence Zones With Ease

A simple dashboard card for Home Assistant that lets you draw room zones around your Aqara FP400 presence sensor and block the "ghost spots" that make lights turn on when nobody is there.

🧭 What This Application Does

The Aqara FP400 is a presence sensor. It watches an area and reports whether a person is inside it. Out of the box, the sensor treats the whole room as one big zone. That means it cannot tell the difference between someone sitting on the sofa and someone standing in the doorway.

This project adds two things to your smart home setup:

  • A dashboard card for Home Assistant. It draws a simple picture of the room, and you click and drag to create zones right on your screen.
  • A converter file for Zigbee2MQTT. It translates the FP400's zone messages into something Home Assistant understands, so each zone becomes its own sensor.

Once set up, you can create an automation such as "turn on the desk lamp when zone 1 detects a person" or "ignore the hallway reading entirely, because a curtain moves there all day long."

🖱️ Drawing Your First Zone

With everything installed, the fun part begins. Open the dashboard where you want the card to live.

  1. Click Edit dashboard in the top right corner, then Add card.
  2. Search the card list for the name shown in the project instructions and select it.
  3. In the card settings, choose your FP400 device from the dropdown list.
  4. Give the card a title, for example "Living Room Zones".
  5. Save the card. It now appears on your dashboard as a room outline.
  6. Click on the outline and drag with your mouse to draw a rectangle. That rectangle is your first zone.
  7. Name the zone something meaningful, such as "Sofa" or "Desk".
  8. Click Save on the card. The zone is written back to the sensor.

Repeat those last three steps for each area of the room you care about. Most rooms only need two or three zones to be genuinely useful.

👻 Blocking Ghost Spots

A "ghost spot" is a spot in the room that keeps reporting a person when there is nobody there. Common causes are a moving curtain near a window, a ceiling fan, a pet bed, or a reflective surface. The card lets you mark these spots so the sensor stops trusting them.

  1. Open the card on your dashboard.
  2. Switch to the blocking view, using the toggle shown in the project instructions.
  3. Draw a shape over the problem area, exactly as you would draw a normal zone.
  4. Save the card. The blocked area is now ignored by your automations.

Give it a day. If the false triggers stop, the block is working. If they return somewhere else, draw another block over the new area.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The card does not appear in the Add card list

HACS sometimes needs a full browser restart rather than a page refresh. Close every browser window on your Windows computer, open the browser again, and sign back in to Home Assistant. Also check that the download in HACS finished and did not stop halfway.

The card loads but shows no room outline

Open the card settings and confirm the correct FP400 device is selected. If the dropdown is empty, the sensor is not yet visible to Home Assistant. Restart Zigbee2MQTT and then restart Home Assistant.

Zones are drawn but no new sensors appear

This almost always means the converter file is in the wrong folder or Zigbee2MQTT has not been restarted since you placed it. Double-check the file location against the project instructions, then restart Zigbee2MQTT and wait a minute for device discovery.

Presence still triggers with nobody in the room

Draw a block over the area that is causing it. Moving curtains and fans are the two most common culprits. If the room has glass or a mirror, blocking the reflection is usually enough.

The dashboard feels slow after adding the card

Keep the number of zones modest. Four to six zones per sensor is plenty for most homes, and a smaller number keeps the dashboard responsive on older tablets and phones.

💡 Tips for Best Results

  • Mount the sensor at the height the manufacturer recommends. A tilted or ceiling-mounted sensor gives zones very different shapes than the manual assumes.
  • Draw zones that cover real furniture. A zone that lines up with the sofa will report presence far more reliably than a zone in open floor space.
  • Name zones after what people do there, not after coordinates. "Reading Chair" is easier to use in an automation six months from now than "Zone 3".
  • Change one thing at a time. Adjust a single zone, then watch your automations for a day before adjusting another.
  • Keep a note of your original layout. If you experiment and dislike the result, you can redraw the previous shapes in a minute.

❓ Questions People Ask

Does this replace the Aqara app?

Yes, for zone configuration. Once the FP400 is paired in Zigbee2MQTT, you manage zones through this card rather than through the manufacturer's phone app.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Everything in this guide happens through menus, buttons, and mouse drags. The converter file is copied into a folder, not edited by hand.

Will this work on a Mac or a phone?

The instructions here focus on Windows because that is the most common desktop setup, but the card itself runs inside the Home Assistant web interface, so it works in any modern browser on any operating system.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The card and the converter run entirely on your own Home Assistant and Zigbee2MQTT machines. Nothing leaves your home network.

Will updates break my zones?

Updates through HACS and the converter are designed to keep your existing configuration. Still, note down your zone names before a major update so you can rebuild them quickly if something unexpected happens.
